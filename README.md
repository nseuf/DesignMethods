### Project 2: The Future of Visual Design
#### Prompt
The year is 2029. You are a designer living in the former Republic of Bezoslavia, western North America.

In the devastating aftermath of the Second Corporate War of Alphabets, the ascendant political regime has banned any and all organizational branding, trademarks, logos, and associated advertising. It has mandated that any packaged products and storefronts can only be labeled with, at most, three English words that specifically describe their contents.

Furthermore, as a concession to the powerful High Church of Imagery, all photorealism is banned for civilian communication purposes. 

The approved forms of visual communication are, according to state law:

Abstract visual forms, designs, and symbols.
Representational symbolism, provided they are not photorealistic.
Typefaces and lettering, provided they do not evoke any trademarks of “old corporate powers”.
Unlimited use of color.
All signs and packages are allotted up to three words each. (Hyphenated and compound words are counted as a single word.)

Despite these draconian restrictions around communications, all pre-war corporations and organizations still operate, and produce their respective goods and services. However, the new rules have left them bewildered as to how to distinguish themselves in the marketplace, or communicate to customers where they are located, and what they sell.

Your task: prepare a design pitch for a surviving organization (aka any 2026 company) by creating a unique visual communication system within state regulations. Specifically, apply the concept to:

A storefront
A product package
At least one other piece relevant collateral (e.g. uniforms, containers, interior signage, stationery, etc.)

### Project 3: Teach Me, Teach You
#### Prompt
Identify a tool that you are interested in developing skills with. This could be design software, a physical tool, or a particular mental activity—as long as it is relevant to your development as a designer, it's fair game. 

Then, in stages: 

Set a SMART goal for what you'd like to accomplish before next week's class. 
For at least 20 minutes, attempt to use the tool with the absolute minimal amount of knowledge needed to safely use the tool. 
Next, seek out a learning resource for that tool, and engage with it in earnest. 
Return to your goal (modified if needed based on your new knowledge), and reach it before the due date.
Once you've completed your task, create another brief reflection on the development of your system image, including visual diagrams of your "before and after" conceptual model.

### Project 5: Cognitive Reloading
#### Prompt
Thanks to the miracles of technology, you’ve extended your capability into the world- navigation, memory, coordination, conversation! Incredible! 

And yet, there are those amongst us who desire to take back certain functions from the machine. Working in groups, you’ll develop a prototype applications that is built to gradually transfer back the cognitive workload to the user.

For example, what would a navigation app look like that was trying to teach you how to navigate better via landmarks? Or a camera app that helps you form durable visual memories? 

Start with creative brainstorming:

List tools that we utilize to extend our conscious capabilities;
Make a note from that list what cognitive processes those tools allow us to offload;
Zero in on one of those cognitive skills that someone might want to reclaim. 
Consider their motivations- what do they really want to gain control over? 
Then, for 20 minutes, individually sketch/prototype some concepts for how this tool might work. 

Finally, rejoin with your group and share the results of your ideation. What do you like? What do you not like? What can you combine, stretch, modify or remix?  Create a shared Figma file that you can collaborate on to create your final prototype, divide the work among your group, and develop your project for presentation by next class.
